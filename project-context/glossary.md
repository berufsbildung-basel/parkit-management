## This glossary provides definitions for key terms and concepts used throughout the project.

###  Project and management terms
**P.A.R.K.I.T** - The parking reservation application in which users select the spots and manage their reservation.

---

**LED Integration** - The planned connection of LED panels to the existing P.A.R.K.I.T application.

---

**Project Goal** - The final result that the project should achieve.

---

**Problem Statement** - A clear description of the problem that the project should solve, for example users choosing the wrong spot.

---

**Proposed Solution** - A possible solution that has not yet been fully approved or implemented.

---

**Stakeholder** - A person or group affected by the project, such as users, developers or administration.

---

**Project Scope** - The work and functionality included in the project.

---

**In Scope** - Tasks and features that are part of the project.

---

**Out of Scope** - Tasks and features that are not part of the current project.

---

**Requirement** - Something the system or project must provide.

---

**Business Requirement** - A requirement describing why the organisation needs the solution.

---

**Constraint** - A limitation that affects the project, such as budget, time or existing hardware.

---

**Assumption** - Something considered true during planning but not yet confirmed.

---

**Dependency** - Something that must be available or completed before another task can begin.

---

**Milestone** - An important checkpoint in the project, such as administration approval.

---

**Risk** - A possible event that could negatively affect the project.

---

**Impact** - The consequences if a risk occurs.

---

**Probability** - How likely it is that a risk will occur.

---

**Risk Mitigation** - An action that reduces the probability or impact of a risk.

---

**Administration Approval** - Formal permission from the administration to continue with the project.

---

**Cost Estimation** - An approximate calculation of hardware, software, installation and maintenance costs.

---

###  Research and development terms

**Research**. - The process of collecting information and comparing possible solutions.

---

**Option Evaluation** - Comparing different solutions using defined criteria.

---

**Decision Criteria** - Factors used to select a solution, such as cost, security, reliability and complexity.

---

**System Architecture** - A high-level description of the system components and how they communicate.

---

**Architecture Option** - One possible technical structure for implementing the solution.

---

**Prototype** - An early working version used to test the main functionality.

---

**Pilot** - A limited real-world installation tested with actual users or panels.

---

**Implementation** - The process of building and introducing the approved solution.

---

**Integration** - Connecting a new component to an existing system.

---

**Testing** - Checking whether the solution works correctly and meets the requirements.

---

**Deployment** - Installing and starting the software in its target environment.

---

**Rollout** - Introducing the finished solution to all planned panels or locations.

---

**Maintenance**. - Work required to keep the system operational after implementation.

---

**Scalability** - The ability to add more panels or users without redesigning the whole system.

---

**Reliability** - The ability of the system to work correctly and consistently.

---

### Software and communication terms

**Rails Application** - The existing web application built with Ruby on Rails.

---

**Backend** - The part of the application that processes data and business logic.

---

**Frontend** -	The part of the application that users see and interact with.

---

**Database** -	The system used to store spots, bookings, content and panel information.

---

**Business Logic** - Rules that determine how bookings, content and panel selection work.

---

**API** - 	A defined interface through which different software components exchange data.

---

**Service** - 	A software component responsible for a specific task, such as sending LED messages.

---

**Message** - 	Data sent from one system component to another.

---

**Payload** - 	The actual content contained inside a message.

---

**Status Message** - 	A message reporting the condition of a panel or controller.

---

**Acknowledgement** - 	Confirmation that a message was received or processed.

---

**Monitoring** -	Continuously checking whether the system and panels are working.

---

**Error Handling** - 	The way the system detects and responds to failures.

---

**Authentication** - 	Verification of the identity of a user, device or application.

---

**Authorization** - 	Determining which actions an authenticated user or device is allowed to perform.

---

### Hardware

**LED Panel** - 	A physical display consisting of multiple LEDs.

---

**LED Matrix** - 	LEDs arranged in rows and columns to display text or graphics.

---

**RGB LED Matrix** - 	An LED matrix capable of displaying different colours using red, green and blue LEDs.

---

**Controller**	 - A device that receives data and controls what is displayed on a panel.

---

**Raspberry Pi** - 	A small computer that could be used as an LED controller.

---

**HUB75**	- A common hardware interface used to connect RGB LED matrix panels to a controller.

---

**Power Supply**	 - A component that provides the electrical power required by the panel and controller.

---

**Network Connection**	 - The wired or wireless connection used by devices to exchange data.

---

**Mounting** - 	The physical installation and attachment of the LED panels.

---

**Hardware Compatibility** - 	The ability of hardware components to work together correctly.

---

**Panel Status** - 	The current condition of a panel, such as online, offline or displaying content.

---

**Offline Mode** - 	Behaviour of the controller or panel when it loses its network connection.
